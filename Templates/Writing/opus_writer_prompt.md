# Claude Opus Master Writer Prompt

Write the final production-ready narration for the selected project using the existing outline, research, approved claims, host identity, templates, runtime target, retention structure, visual cues, cautions, and CTA rules.

Do not research again, add sources, add or strengthen medical claims, invent credentials, or imply the virtual host is a licensed clinician.

## Approved Depth and No-Padding Rule

Develop every already-approved outline beat to its full legitimate depth. A no-padding instruction does not mean compressing or summarizing approved material. Reach the configured runtime by fully developing approved distinctions, mechanisms, comparisons, observation prompts, evidence context, and illustrative stories when the outline authorizes them.

Do not reach runtime through repetition, paraphrased restatement, new claims, new mechanisms, new statistics, new evidence, new remedies, or invented examples that exceed the approved outline and Medical Gate 1 boundaries. If the outline contains enough approved material for the configured runtime, develop that material rather than declaring the target unsupported merely because a compressed draft runs short.

## Spoken Safety Budget Preservation

Treat the outline's narrated safety/disclaimer placement plan as a binding structural constraint. Medical Gate 1 cautions, prohibited claims, approved-claim boundaries, and non-spoken writer constraints are constraints on what you may say; they are not instructions to verbalize those boundaries throughout the narration.

If the outline permits one or two narrated safety/disclaimer locations, generic defensive safety language may appear only in those permitted locations. Outside them, do not add or paraphrase generic statements whose function is to say that something is not a diagnosis, not self-diagnosis, not proof, not confirmed, not established, not identified, not settled, not a verdict, not a label, not a test, or not a scoring system. Likewise, do not add generic reminders not to diagnose, assume, guess, treat, change medication, or seek/consult a professional outside the outline's permitted safety location unless the outline explicitly places that exact safety action there.

This restriction applies to the hook, section openings, explanations, observation prompts, hypothetical stories, recaps, loop closures, transitions, pattern interrupts, teasers, practical actions, final payoff, and CTA. Do not turn non-spoken constraints into spoken reassurance or defensive narration merely to sound careful.

Claim-specific scientific qualification may remain outside the consolidated safety location only when it is necessary for factual accuracy of that specific claim and is already authorized by the outline or evidence package. Examples include population, dose, duration, comparator, study design, association-versus-causation wording, and named-study scope. State such a limitation once where needed. Do not attach a generic diagnosis/proof/verdict disclaimer to it.

Medication restrictions, referral/escalation language, red flags, and urgent-help wording must remain in the outline-designated safety location unless the outline explicitly requires otherwise. Preserve safety by obeying the non-spoken constraints and prohibited-claim boundaries, not by repeatedly narrating them.

Before finalizing `06_final_script.md`, silently scan all spoken narration for semantic safety/disclaimer leakage outside the permitted location(s). Wording changes do not evade this rule: `not a diagnosis`, `not a verdict`, `does not prove`, `does not establish`, `does not identify`, `does not settle the cause`, `not a self-diagnosis checklist`, and semantic equivalents all consume the same safety budget. If leakage exists, rewrite the sentence positively or remove it before delivery. Do not claim in `06_medical_review.md` that the spoken-safety budget passed unless the actual narration passes this semantic scan.

## Required Separate Outputs

Create exactly these separate files:

1. `06_final_script.md` — narration only, with title, section headings, spoken narration, `[Visual Cue: ...]` blocks, cautions, and CTA. Do not include QA reports, scorecards, runtime tables, configuration notes, or process commentary.
2. `06_runtime_report.md` — word count, estimated narration runtime, target comparison, section runtimes, and assumed WPM.
3. `06_retention_report.md` — hook, open loops, pattern interrupts, section teasers, payoff, CTA placement, and retention risks.
4. `06_medical_review.md` — claims-preserved check, safety language, cautions, unsupported-claim check, and an accurate spoken-safety-budget check against the actual narration. This is an editorial review, not a replacement for Medical Gate 2.
5. `06_humanization_report.md` — conversational score, host-voice score, emotional connection, repeated phrasing, contractions, rhetorical questions, and largest humanization changes.

Only narration may remain in `06_final_script.md`.

Use natural educator-to-viewer language, clear transitions, occasional rhetorical questions, concise paragraphs, and clearly illustrative stories. Preserve all approved medical meaning and safety caveats while keeping non-spoken boundaries non-spoken unless the outline explicitly assigns them to narration.
